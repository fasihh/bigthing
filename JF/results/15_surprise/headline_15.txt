JF 15 · Surprise-tempered PoE pilot (Kaggle, CPU, commit 9228595, 3 seeds, frozen models, no retraining)

Method: each expert's latent belief is compared with the leave-one-out PoE of the other experts.
Precision is scaled by (z/c)^-k above the clean-train 99th percentile c. Temperature on clean valid;
k tuned on valid with self-made corruptions (noise lambda=1, random swap). Held-out at test: noise
lambda=0.5, dead sensor, two noisy inputs.

Reading
- Works where the experts share a latent space in which disagreement is meaningful (contrastive
  "align" experts on COCO): every corruption kind improves, including held-out ones, at zero clean
  cost. Uncertainty-based detection of corrupted clips 0.56 -> 0.84; Cov90 0.84 -> 0.89; TR 0.88 -> 0.95.
- With V-JEPA experts it does almost nothing: the surprise score separates corrupted clips only
  weakly (COCO 0.70, MOSEI 0.39), so the weights barely move. The hypothesis "V-JEPA's own
  cross-modal predictions give a free surprise signal" is NOT supported in this form.
- MOSEI: small gains only (align swap 1.450->1.404; vjepa_vt dead 1.491->1.449), because the surprise
  score is near chance on MOSEI (0.39-0.58).

Novelty check (28 Nov)
- Down-weighting experts that disagree with the others is known: generalised PoE (Joshi et al. 2022,
  trained credibility nets), CLoE (arXiv 2603.09316, agreement -> trained gate weights),
  reliability-weighted PoE (arXiv 2610.29621, learned r_m), and chi^2 innovation gating in sensor
  fusion (Kalman NIS). Our variant is training-free, uses a leave-one-out PoE in the shared latent,
  and thresholds from clean data only. That is an incremental, post-hoc variant, not a new idea.
- The useful finding for Part II is the conditional one: it only helps when the expert latents are
  aligned (contrastive), not with V-JEPA experts as they stand.
